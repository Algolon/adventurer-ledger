# M1.4 rules-engine coverage

## Outcome

M1.4 closes the silent-effect, dice/roll, equipment-bundle, and baseline multiclass gaps with additive schema-v2 fields. It does not convert or include private content. Existing schema-v2 documents remain valid: new entry collections default to empty, new progression collections default to empty, and new effect variants are opt-in.

M1.4 is a rules-engine and content-resolution foundation. It is not the M2.1 product slice: there is no character persistence, no character service, no play sheet, no level-up workflow, and no transfer workflow in this milestone.

## Effect capability matrix

The machine-readable source of truth is `src/rules/effect-capabilities.ts`. TypeScript requires every member of `Effect["type"]` to occur in that map, and the engine uses an exhaustive switch. Golden tests require every mapped variant to produce an explicit runtime trace, and `tests/effect-semantics.test.ts` asserts the resulting state and issues per variant rather than only the trace.

| Schema support | Runtime disposition | Effect variants |
| --- | --- | --- |
| supported | automatic | `grantProficiency`, `grantExpertise`, `grantFeature`, `replaceFeature`, `disableFeature`, `modifyAbility`, `modifyAbilityMaximum`, `modifySkill`, `modifySavingThrow`, `modifyArmorClass`, `modifyInitiative`, `modifySpeed`, `modifyCriticalRange`, `modifyAttack`, `modifyDamage`, `addSpell`, `addSpellList`, `addResource`, `addAttack`, `addAction`, `addBonusAction`, `addReaction`, `setMinimum`, `setMaximum`, `setCalculation`, `addAdvantage`, `addDisadvantage`, `rechargeOnShortRest`, `rechargeOnLongRest`, `unlockAtLevel`, `scaleAtLevel`, `addWeaponMastery`, `grantFightingStyle`, `grantManeuver`, `grantInvocation`, `grantMetamagic`, `addDice`, `replaceDice`, `rerollDice`, `setMinimumRoll`, `grantEquipmentBundle` |
| supported | choice-driven | `grantChoice` |
| supported | manual-adjudication / review-required | `manualAdjudication` |

There are 43 allowed variants: 41 automatic, one choice-driven, and one explicitly manual. A condition or level gate that does not match is traced as such. Choice-driven and manual effects create visible structured issues. Evaluation failure is an error. No valid effect can fall through as “applied” without a handler.

## Bounding operation semantics

`Operation` contains `min` and `max`. They name the **bound they establish**, not the arithmetic they perform:

| Operation | Meaning | Implementation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `min` | Raise the value to **at least** the operand (lower bound) | `Math.max(current, operand)` |
| `max` | Cap the value at **at most** the operand (upper bound) | `Math.min(current, operand)` |

This is the same semantics as the `setMinimum` and `setMaximum` effect variants, and `tests/effect-semantics.test.ts` asserts that the two spellings agree in all four directions. A value with no prior state adopts the operand. The declaration in `src/domain/model.ts` carries the same note, so the names cannot silently drift away from the behaviour.

## Action-economy grants

`addAttack`, `addAction`, `addBonusAction` and `addReaction` do not collapse into one untyped list. The runtime produces a single typed grant collection, `RuleResult.actionGrants`, whose members are `{ kind: "attack" | "action" | "bonus-action" | "reaction"; definitionId; effectId }`. `actionGrantsByKind` returns the deterministic per-kind view the Actions surface in `docs/product/MOBILE_SHEET_SPEC.md` needs.

Ordering follows effect priority and then effect ID. A repeated `(kind, definitionId)` pair is granted once; the same definition used in two different categories stays two grants.

## Dice and rolls

The runtime records extra dice, dice replacement, bounded rerolls, minimum rolls, critical-range changes, advantage/disadvantage, and conditional modifiers as structured derived state. It does not roll dice. Targets and selectors remain declarative identifiers; consumers decide when a registered rule applies. Dice expressions and selectors are copied into the result, so later mutation of an effect object cannot change already-derived state.

## Equipment bundles

Entries may define first-class bundles containing fixed items, nested bundles, nested choices, quantities, alternatives, and `granted`, `carried`, or `equipped` status. Backgrounds opt in through the new `equipmentBundleIds`; the existing `equipmentChoiceIds` keeps its previous aggregate-entry meaning. Bundle resolution is pure and deterministic. Import-set preview and confirmation both block missing bundle and item references, including broken alternatives. Currency alternatives are represented but remain a choice for the builder rather than being silently selected.

Bundle status stays in the pure resolution output (`ResolvedEquipmentItem.status`). `CharacterInventoryItem` is unchanged and keeps `equipped` as its single writable carry state, so there is no second persisted representation that could contradict it. M1.4 adds no character persistence migration; a persisted carry-state model belongs to the M2.1 persistence review.

## Derived character scope

`deriveCharacterState` is the pure boundary that resolves the typed schema relations, so M2.1 does not reimplement schema traversal. It resolves:

- class and subclass progression by class level, starting and multiclass proficiencies, multiclass prerequisites and unsupported combinations, and combined spell-slot contribution;
- species `speed` and `traitIds`, legacy race `speed` and `traitIds`, lineage `traitIds`, lineage `replacesTraitIds`, and lineage/species parentage;
- background `proficiencyIds`, background `featId`, and background `equipmentBundleIds`;
- selected-entry effects, prerequisites, nested choices, and unresolved required choices;
- equipment-bundle resolution and unresolved equipment choices.

Species speed is the base value that `modifySpeed` then modifies. Traits replaced by a lineage are neither activated nor allowed to contribute effects. Unavailable traits, unavailable background feats, mismatched selection categories, invalid identity mechanics, and a lineage that does not belong to the selected species are machine-readable issues (`FEATURE_REFERENCE_MISSING`, `IDENTITY_MECHANICS_INVALID`, `LINEAGE_INVALID`), not silent omissions.

Out of scope here and owned by M2.1: automatic sheet calculations (AC, hit points, attack and save expressions), character services, persistence, overrides, and every UI surface.

## Import-set confirmation boundary

Every confirmed import goes through one boundary, `commitImportStates`, inside a single flat Dexie transaction:

1. assert that every record each preview observed is unchanged;
2. rebuild the **complete** set preview against confirmation-time database state — per-document checks plus all cross-file dependency, reference, progression, choice-target, equipment, alias and conflict checks;
3. only then write packs, sources, entries and version history, dependencies first.

Any stale observation, failed revalidation, cancellation, or storage failure rolls back every pack, source, entry and history record in the set. Failure is a typed `ImportConfirmationError` with `code` (`PREVIEW_INVALID`, `PREVIEW_STALE`, `SET_REVALIDATION_FAILED`) and sanitized `issues` carrying codes, stable IDs and field paths only.

`confirmImport` revalidates its single document as a one-file import set, so a single-file import cannot bypass the set-level reference guarantees. `previewContentPack` remains the per-document preview primitive and carries no cross-file guarantee; the content workspace previews and confirms through the set functions so what the user sees is what confirmation revalidates. No nested Dexie transaction is opened, and a test asserts that a multi-file confirmation enters exactly one transaction scope.

`tests/import-set-atomicity.test.ts` covers a removed dependency pack, a removed required linked entry, a removed class-progression target, a removed choice target, a changed installed conflict policy, a later-file write failure after an earlier file would have written, cancellation between files, an atomic multi-file success, and the single-file boundary.

## Multiclass semantics

Class progression levels are class levels. `Character.level` is total level. Class mechanics can declare starting proficiencies, multiclass prerequisites, multiclass-only proficiency grants, spell-slot progression (`none`, `full`, `half`, `third`, or `pact`), explicit up/down contribution rounding, and unsupported class combinations. Subclass and progression choices are gated by class level. Combined full/half/third caster slots use the standard level table; pact slots stay separate and produce `review-required` when combined with other spellcasting.

Unsupported or inconsistent states are machine-readable: level-sum mismatch, unavailable class/features, invalid subclass, failed prerequisite, unsupported combination, missing choice/equipment, separate pact slots, manual effect, and effect failure.

## Pack completeness and compatibility

`pilot`, `partial`, and `complete` remain unchanged. Pilot and partial packs show an incomplete-source label in the content workspace and always produce `PACK_INCOMPLETE` during preview. The identity guard still prevents a pilot identity from claiming complete coverage.

Schema version remains `2`. Existing effects retain their prior shapes and new fields use backwards-compatible defaults. No migration is needed because no stored field became required. Dexie records created before M1.4 may omit `equipmentBundles`; runtime and export treat that as an empty list. No character table changed, so no character migration is required.

## Remaining limitations

- Pact Magic slot quantities and refresh behavior are deliberately separate from combined multiclass slots.
- Known/prepared spell merging and per-class spellcasting ability selection need a spellcasting vertical slice.
- Currency alternatives are modeled but require a builder selection surface.
- Complex spell area/target/upcast idioms and richer monster action/recharge grammar remain outside this slice.
- Errata/reprint policy still needs a real controlled multi-edition pilot.
- The private Brammel mechanics still require independent rules review before being declared a canonical implementation.
- The private Brammel pilot was **not** rerun for M1.4. `tests/private-ingestion.local.test.ts` is skipped unless `ADVENTURER_LEDGER_PRIVATE_PACK` is supplied, so no claim in this document is backed by a private-pilot run.
- Background `equipmentChoiceIds` keeps its aggregate-entry meaning and is validated as a reference, not resolved into items.

## Gates

- M1.4 rules-engine and content-resolution foundation: **GO**. Exhaustive effect dispositions, bounded roll state, pure choice/equipment/derivation layers, and the atomic import-set confirmation boundary are implemented and covered.
- Synthetic engine and import regression coverage: **GO**. All assertions use original synthetic fixtures; failure, rollback, stale-state, cancellation and sanitized-diagnostic paths are included.
- Functional M2.1 product slice: **NO-GO**. M1.4 implements no character persistence, service, sheet, level-up or transfer surface. The slice stays NO-GO until M2.1 implements and verifies the accepted automatic-sheet contract in `docs/product/M2_ACCEPTANCE_CRITERIA.md` and `docs/product/M2_SERVICE_BOUNDARIES.md`.
- Full PHB 2024 conversion: **NO-GO** pending the spellcasting, monster, errata, and independent-review gaps above.
- Multiclass character creation: **NO-GO for general release**; baseline prerequisites, progressions, proficiencies, subclass gates, and combined slots are covered, but pact and known/prepared-spell semantics still require vertical-slice validation.
